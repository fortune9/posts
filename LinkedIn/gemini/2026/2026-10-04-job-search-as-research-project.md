🔬 **What if you treated your job search like a research project?**

As a scientist, I’m used to starting with a goal, making a plan, gathering materials, choosing methods, running experiments, analyzing results, and adjusting based on what I learn, and reiterate for better.

I’ve realized a job search can work the same way.

🎯 **The goal:** Find a role that fits.

🧰 **The materials:** My resume, experience, skills, network, and a list of potential employers.

🧪 **The methods:** Tailor my resume, research roles, set job alerts, and reach out to people in my network.

📊 **The results:** Often, no response or a polite rejection. Those outcomes are expected in a process with many variables—and they can still teach me something.

Each application is a chance to review the approach and make a thoughtful adjustment. Interviews offer even more to learn: from other people’s experience, from the questions they ask, and from the gaps I discover in my own knowledge.

An offer may be the eventual outcome. But progress is already happening along the way: I’m growing my technical knowledge, sharpening my communication, and setting new learning goals.

I used to send out a resume and wait. Treating the search as a project has helped me engage with it actively and learn from each step. I’ll share more of those lessons as I go.

**What have you learned from your own job search? I’d love to hear in the comments.** 👇
